# S1: Expression model and temporal resolution

**Verdict: GO on expressiveness (2026-11-24).** Both apps' old and new rules are encoded as JSON rule packs and evaluated by one engine, with no app-specific rule code and no fallback. **Correctness against attested ground truth is not yet checked.** The examples that pass are agent-suggested (see "Not verified").

## What was built
- `packages/core`: rule pack schema and validation (`model.ts`), evaluator with trace and unsupported results (`evaluator.ts`), temporal resolver (`temporal.ts`), citation check (`citations.ts`), date helpers (`dates.ts`). Node-only helpers (`node/`) load snapshots and run pack checks. They are not exported from the package index.
- `apps/us-tax-estimator/rules/pack.json`: 3 rule versions, 7 citations. Standard deduction original and revised, 2025 single-filer brackets.
- `apps/jp-child-allowance/rules/pack.json`: 6 rule versions, 18 citations. Pre-reform (snapshot 2023-12-04) and post-reform (snapshot 2024-07-31) monthly allowance, income limits and cap, payments per year.
- `spikes/s1/build-packs.mjs` generates both pack files. The JSON is the artifact. The script only avoids hand-writing nested expressions.
- Size: core about 830 non-test lines (evaluator 347, model 301, helpers the rest). The JP pack is 591 pretty-printed JSON lines, the US pack 176.

## Exit criterion
| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| US 2025 original and revised encoded and evaluated | Yes. Same filer gives $15,000 deduction and tax $3,961.50 as of 2025-06-30, and $15,750 and $3,871.50 as of 2025-12-31. |
| JP pre- and post-reform encoded and evaluated | Yes. Same three children give 35,000 yen (old rules) and 50,000 yen (new rules). The third child moves from 15,000 to 30,000. |
| No app-specific rule code | Met. Child order, age windows, sibling counting up to age 22, income test removal, and progressive bands are all expressed in the pack. |
| At most 3 operators added to the initial set | Met: one, `period`. Other additions are schema, not operators: `knownFromBasis`, scope `period`, fact `default`, `excludeMonthDays`, `uniqueBy`, `assumptions`. |

## Verified
- `npm test`: 37 tests pass across 6 files. `npm run typecheck` passes.
- All 25 citations (7 US, 18 JP) are verified against the committed snapshots by hash and by normalized text. Test `pack-files.test.ts` proves the harness fails on an altered quote and on a wrong rule value.
- Citation check unit tests cover Japanese, English, full-width spaces (NFKC), quotes spanning line breaks, near-miss quotes that must fail, hash mismatch, wrong source, and trivial quotes.
- US bracket arithmetic reproduces the "plus" amounts printed in IRB 2024-45 Table 3 at the 22% threshold ($5,578.50) and at the top threshold ($188,769.75). These are candidates `us-3` and `us-5`.
- Temporal behavior: with knowledge dated 2025-06-30 the original deduction applies. From 2025-07-04 the revised one applies. For JP, October 2024 evaluated with knowledge from 2024-06-30 uses the old rules, because old versions are open-ended and the new version only supersedes once known (candidate `jp-12`).
- Unsupported results (never a number): other filing status, 65 or older, other year, unknown fact, nothing known yet, twins, April 1 birth date, seven children, six dependents, mid-month period. All return typed reason codes.
- Rev. Proc. 2025-32 section 3 (2025 items modified) removes only sections 2.15(1) (standard deduction) and 2.25 (section 179) of Rev. Proc. 2024-40. It does not remove 2.01 (tax rate tables). This is the basis for leaving the 2025 brackets as one version. Checked by searching the snapshot.

## Not verified
- **Correctness against ground truth.** The 28 agent-suggested cases in `ground-truth-candidates/` (10 US, 18 JP) are derived by me from the same sources. They are not independent and not attested. Task 7.3 (human) is the real check.
- **Interpretations the citations do not settle** (quotes prove existence, not meaning):
  - The removal of the income cap after the reform is inferred from the page telling people over the old cap that they must apply. The page does not state it plainly.
  - The age-3 step uses completed years on the first day of the month. Whether the step happens in the birthday month or the next is not checked.
  - "Third child onward" counts older children strictly by birth date. Twins are unsupported.
  - Children born on April 1 are unsupported. The pre-reform page says "誕生日後" while the post-reform page says "達する日以後". The two differ by one day only for that date.
  - The oldest eligible child at the age edges (the month the window closes) is not checked.
- JP rules before 2023-12 and after the retrieval date are not modeled. `appliesFrom 2023-12-01` is the earliest evidenced month, not the legal start.
- US: wages are treated as taxable income before the standard deduction. No credits, FICA, or other income. The estimate is for demonstration.

## Findings
1. **Authoring cost is the main weakness.** Nested JSON expressions are verbose and hard to read in review. The JP pack needed a generator script. If the skeleton is meant for other developers, a small builder or a readable text form is worth adding. This is outside S1 and not planned. It matters for how the project is described.
2. **`knownFromBasis` works.** The Crew review asked what `knownFrom` means. Each version now says `enactment`, `publication`, or `snapshot-upper-bound`. The revised US deduction uses `enactment` (2025-07-04) with a second citation to the sentence "The OBBBA was signed into law on July 4, 2025." from the same bulletin. The original uses `publication` (the bulletin date 2024-11-04), which is conservative: the Rev. Proc. says its figures reflect law as of 2024-10-22. JP versions use `snapshot-upper-bound`.
3. **Scope is where safety lives.** Most of the risk handling is declarative scope: allowed values, exclusions, uniqueness, and a period range. The evaluator rejects anything outside it before any rule runs.
4. **Per-rule versioning kept the US change small.** Only `standardDeductionCents` has two versions. The bracket rule is unchanged and unaware of the revision.
5. **Tooling.** TypeScript 7 defaults `types` to empty, so `@types/node` needs `"types": ["node"]` in the core tsconfig. Zod 4 is used for the envelope. Expression trees are validated by hand-written code, not Zod, to keep recursive errors readable.

## Decisions
- Keep the expression tree JSON, with the one added operator (`period`). No fallback needed.
- Keep `knownFromBasis` in the schema.
- Boundary cases that cannot be resolved from the sources are excluded in scope, not guessed.
